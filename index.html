From Australia’s Favourite Meat to a Multi-Billion-Dollar Industry

The Chicken Nation

Fifty years ago, beef dominated Australia's dinner table. Today, more chicken is available per Australian than pork and beef combined. This is the story of how chicken became Australia's favourite meat, how its industry grew, and where poultry production is concentrated.

01

How Chicken Won Australia's Dinner Table

In 2024-2025, chicken accounted for almost 54 kg of apparent meat consumption per person - nearly twice the amount of pork and almost three times that of beef.

It was not always so. In the mid-1970s the average Australian ate more than 60 kg of beef a year and only about 12 kg of chicken. Over the following five decades the two lines crossed. Chicken was always ahead of pork and lamb; beef was its only serious rival.

In fifty years, chicken consumption rose more than four-fold while beef fell by about two thirds.

Chicken's rise coincided with a substantial relative price advantage. Since 1980-1981 its consumer price index has roughly doubled, compared with increases of around five-fold for beef, lamb and consumer prices overall. This suggests that relative affordability may have contributed to chicken's growing popularity, although price alone cannot explain the change.

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) tracks how the price of the same item changes over time. Each line is set to 100 in 1980-1981, the first year with data for all four meats, so the end value shows how many times dearer each item is today. The dashed line is the average of all prices, a measure of general inflation. Source: ABS Consumer Price Index, as published in ABARES, Agricultural Commodity Statistics 2025, Table 12.3.

02

The Rise of a Multi-Billion-Dollar Industry

Chicken leads Australia's meat consumption, but poultry does not dominate the livestock economy. In 2024-2025, poultry sold for meat generated $4.1 billion in local value, ranking fourth among the six sectors examined. Yet it recorded the fastest nominal value growth between 2020-2021 and 2024-2025.

Fastest growth of the six sectors

Poultry sales rose 42% in four years.

From $2.88 billion in 2020-2021 to $4.10 billion in 2024-2025. Cattle rose 39%, pigs 30%, sheep 29% and milk 27%, while wool fell 10%. These are nominal dollar values, so part of each increase reflects higher prices rather than more animals.

Between 2020-2021 and 2024-2025, total livestock value increased by $8.6 billion. Cattle contributed the largest increase ($4.77 billion), while poultry added $1.22 billion - approximately 14% of the total growth. Wool was the only sector to decline.

Dotted lines connect each bar to the next. Cattle dominates because it is by far the largest sector. Source: ABS, Australian Agriculture: Livestock, 2024-2025, Tables 1 and 2.

Of the $36.4 billion earned from livestock in 2024-2025, poultry meat made up 11.3%. Animals sold for meat accounted for more than three quarters of the total; products from living animals (milk and wool), made up the rest.

"Poultry (meat)" counts birds sold for meat only; eggs are not included in these 2024-2025 tables. Local value is the value of production at the farm gate, after marketing costs such as freight. Source: ABS, Australian Agriculture: Livestock, 2024-2025, Tables 1 and 2.

Where Australia's Poultry Industry Takes Shape

Australia's poultry industry is geographically uneven. Published regional values highlight concentrations around major cities, while state-level figures show the economic importance of eastern Australia. Comparing chicken meat and eggs reveals distinct geographical patterns, although some regional figures are confidential.

Published regional data reveal high poultry value density around Melbourne, Sydney and Brisbane, particularly in several outer metropolitan areas.

04

Six Decades of Expansion and Change

Chicken output has grown in almost every year for six decades. Since 1966-1967 the number of chickens processed has fallen in only nine years, and never by more than about 7% nationally.

A rare setback

2017-2018 is the only recent year when Victoria and Queensland both fell by more than 10%.

NSW grew 6.5% that year, so the national total fell by just 2.6%. The data show where production moved, not why.

05

Beyond the State Borders: Australia's Poultry Hotspots

Earlier, the regional map highlighted where poultry value is concentrated per square kilometre. But does the region with the highest density also generate the greatest total value? Comparing total regional values reveals a different perspective.

Riverina recorded $305 million in local poultry value in 2020-2021, the highest among regions with published figures. Together with Darling Downs-Maranoa and Geelong, the top three regions accounted for 29% of the published regional total. This highlights how total regional value and production value density can tell different geographical stories.

Hollow dots mark estimates the ABS rates as less reliable. Shares are of the $2.06 billion total for the 56 regions with published figures; the 14 SA and WA regions are confidential and excluded. Source: ABS, Value of Agricultural Commodities Produced, 2020-2021.

Three things to take away

Chicken became Australia's leading meat

Chicken overtook beef in the early 2000s. Over the following decades, its apparent consumption continued to rise, alongside a comparatively smaller increase in consumer prices.

Poultry is growing rapidly in economic value

Poultry sold for meat generated $4.1 billion in 2024-2025, representing 11.3% of the livestock value examined and a 42% nominal increase since 2020-2021.

Poultry's economic footprint is geographically concentrated

New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland accounted for 72% of national poultry disposal value in 2024-2025. At the regional level, Riverina recorded the highest published poultry-meat value in 2020-2021.

Definitions and method

Local value
Value of production at the farm gate, after marketing costs. Nominal dollars, not adjusted for inflation.
np (not published)
The ABS hides figures that could reveal a single business. Shown in grey, never estimated, and distinct from zero.
Price index
Each CPI series ÷ its 1980-1981 value x 100.
Density
Region's production value ÷ its land area in km².
Yearly change
(this year ÷ last year - 1) x 100.
Financial year
1 July to 30 June; quarterly figures are summed only when all four quarters exist.
